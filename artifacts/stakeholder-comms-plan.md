# Stakeholder Communications Plan Template

Built once at program kickoff, revisited at each phase gate. The core idea: match the format and frequency to what each stakeholder group actually needs to act, not one broadcast to everyone.

| Stakeholder group | What they need | Format | Cadence | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Executive sponsor | Trajectory vs. committed date, decisions needed | 1-page status report | Weekly | Program lead |
| Engineering / IT | Detailed technical status, blockers, dependencies | Standup + written sprint notes | Daily / per sprint | Tech lead |
| Compliance / Regulatory | Audit-trail evidence, control validation status | Formal review packet | Per phase gate | Compliance liaison |
| Site / operational teams | What's changing for them, when, what to expect | Training sessions + walkthrough docs | Pre-rollout, then as-needed | Change management lead |
| Cross-functional partners (Finance, Supply Chain, etc.) | Integration points that affect their systems | Working session + shared RAID log | Bi-weekly | Program lead |

## Escalation path

Defined once, in writing, before it's needed: issue → program lead → sponsor → steering committee, with explicit severity thresholds for what jumps straight to sponsor-level (anything that threatens the compliance sign-off date or a regulatory deadline skips the queue).

## Why this matters

The most common failure mode I've seen on regulated, multi-stakeholder programs isn't a missing status update — it's the wrong stakeholder finding out about a risk from someone other than the program lead. A comms plan that's explicit about who hears what, how, and how often prevents that.
